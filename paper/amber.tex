\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present \textsc{amber}, a format and protocol that turn the one-time cost of
embedding a corpus into a single portable artifact that is a cryptographic
commitment to the exact corpus and embedder that produced it. The artifact is
the image of a document set under a pinned, deterministic embedding map, and the
committed root is a public, regenerable witness to that map. Trust in the banked
compute is gated by two independently verifiable predicates. An integrity check
recomputes the commitment in $O(n)$ hashing and needs no model. A probabilistic
\emph{audit} re-embeds a random sample of $k \ll n$ chunks and shows that the
stored vectors are the faithful image of the source under the pinned map. We
formalize the embedder as a deterministic map quantized to a fixed-point
lattice, give the commitment with explicit domain separation, and prove a
sequence of propositions that bound an adversary's advantage by the sample size
and the tamper fraction. We separate what the artifact binds, namely corpus
identity and embedding fidelity, from what it does not. It carries no claim about
any downstream generator, and we prove the intuitive reading that ``the artifact
\emph{is} the answer'' void. We state the guarantee against an explicit hierarchy
of adversary classes and identify the exact anchor at which corpus authenticity,
as opposed to embedding authenticity, becomes external.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Embedding a corpus is a bank-once, use-many computation. The heavy pass over
documents runs once, and the resulting vectors are reused for offline retrieval.
The banked result is portable and works without a network; only a light runtime
remains at task time. A portable artifact that one did not produce oneself raises
a question the retrieval literature rarely asks. Were the banked vectors honestly
computed? A consumer holding a stored index has no model loaded and cannot, in
general, tell faithful embeddings from fabricated ones.

\textsc{amber} composes the two so that the banked artifact is a structural
commitment to the computation that produced it, not just a container of vectors.
The design objective is a single invariant.
\begin{quote}
\itshape (I) Trust in a banked artifact requires the conjunction of an integrity
witness over its committed bytes and a faithful-embedding witness sampled from
the source. A self-consistent artifact alone is insufficient, and either witness
alone is insufficient for the adversary classes of \S\ref{sec:adv}.
\end{quote}
Realizing (I) needs care, because the intuitive reading, that the artifact
\emph{is} the knowledge, is provably void (Proposition~\ref{prop:void}). Our
contribution is a construction that delivers verifiable, reproducible,
offline-portable banked compute, reduces its guarantee to standard hash
assumptions, and gives a precise account of the boundary of that guarantee.

\subsection{Notation and primitives}
We write $\|$ for byte concatenation, $\mathsf{H}$ for \textsc{sha-256}, and
$|x|$ for byte length. A corpus $\mathcal{D}$ is an ordered set of sources. An
embedder $E:\Sigma^\ast\to\mathbb{R}^d$ maps text to a unit vector; $Q$ denotes
fixed-point quantization to $\mathbb{Z}_{[-127,127]}^d$ and $Q^{-1}$ its
dequantization. A chunker $C$ deterministically segments $\mathcal{D}$ into an
ordered list of chunks. We write $\mathrm{negl}(\lambda)$ for a negligible
function of the security parameter $\lambda$, here the hash output length.

\section{System overview}
\textsc{amber} is stratified into three layers with minimal coupling, summarized
in Table~\ref{tab:layers}. The corpus is public and regenerable; the bank is the
frozen embedding compute; the commitment is a succinct public root that binds the
two. The decomposition is the source of the security argument: authenticity is
confined to two scalars, the commitment root $r$ and an external corpus anchor
$r_{\mathcal D}$.

\section{The chunker and embedder}
\begin{construction}[Deterministic chunker]
Normalize each source by \textsc{nfc} and whitespace collapse, then emit fixed
word-count windows of size $w$ with overlap $o<w$, in source order. The
parameters $(w,o,\text{norm})$ are serialized canonically and hashed; the digest
enters the manifest, so two builders with the same corpus and parameters emit
byte-identical chunks.
\end{construction}

\begin{construction}[Pinned embedder]
\label{con:embed}
The embedder's identity and configuration are serialized to a canonical
descriptor whose digest is committed. The reference default is a signed
feature-hashing map: tokens are hashed (keyed \textsc{blake2b}) to a bucket and
sign, term frequencies accumulate, and the vector is $\ell_2$-normalized. It is
dependency-free and bitwise reproducible. A semantic transformer is a drop-in
under Construction~\ref{con:repro}.
\end{construction}

\section{The commitment}
\begin{definition}[Leaf]
For chunk $c_i$ and quantized vector $q_i$ in a sequence of length $n$, the leaf
payload is $p_i = \mathrm{canon}(c_i)\,\|\,\texttt{0x1f}\,\|\,q_i$, and the leaf
hash binds position and length,
\[ \ell_i = \mathsf{H}\!\left(\texttt{0x00}\,\|\,i\,\|\,n\,\|\,|p_i|\,\|\,p_i\right). \]
\end{definition}
Internal nodes use a distinct tag $\texttt{0x01}$, so a leaf can never be
reinterpreted as a node. The root $r=\mathrm{MR}(\ell_0,\dots,\ell_{n-1})$ is the
commitment. A separate root $r_{\mathcal D}$ over $\mathrm{canon}(c_i)$ alone
fixes corpus identity independently of the embedder.

\begin{definition}[Artifact]
An artifact is the tuple $A=(\textsf{manifest}, (c_i), (q_i))$ serialized as a
single file, where the manifest carries the embedder and chunker digests, $d$,
$n$, $r$, and $r_{\mathcal D}$.
\end{definition}

\section{Reproducibility under quantization}
\label{sec:repro}
Floating-point embedding is not guaranteed bitwise-stable across hardware, so a
naive commitment to raw floats would be unverifiable in practice. We commit to
quantized vectors and verify within a tolerance.
\begin{construction}[$\tau$-reproducibility]
\label{con:repro}
$E$ is $\tau$-reproducible if for the pinned configuration and any chunk $c$,
two evaluations $q,q'$ satisfy $\|q-q'\|_\infty\le\tau$. The deterministic
default achieves $\tau=0$; a pinned transformer revision achieves a small $\tau$
absorbed by $Q$.
\end{construction}

\section{Verification and audit}
\begin{proposition}[Integrity]
\label{prop:integrity}
If the stored $(c_i,q_i)$ differ in any byte from those committed by $r$, then
recomputing the root yields $r'\neq r$ except with probability
$\mathrm{negl}(\lambda)$ under collision resistance of $\mathsf{H}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A differing artifact that recomputes to the same root yields, by the standard
Merkle extractor walking from root to the changed leaf, a collision of
$\mathsf{H}$ on tagged, length-bound inputs. Domain separation prevents
leaf and node confusion. \end{proof}
Integrity is cheap and model-free, but it does not establish that the $q_i$ were
honestly produced by $E$. A forger may commit self-consistent fabricated
vectors. The audit closes this gap.

\begin{protocol}[Audit]
Sample $k$ leaf indices uniformly without replacement using OS entropy. For each
sampled $i$, recompute $\hat q_i = Q(E(c_i))$ and declare leaf $i$
\emph{inconsistent} if $\|\hat q_i - q_i\|_\infty>\tau$. Reject if any sampled
leaf is inconsistent.
\end{protocol}

\begin{proposition}[Audit soundness]
\label{prop:audit}
Let $m$ of the $n$ leaves be inconsistent with the pinned $E$ at tolerance
$\tau$, and let $\rho=m/n$. The audit rejects with probability
\[ 1-\frac{\binom{n-m}{k}}{\binom{n}{k}} \;\ge\; 1-(1-\rho)^k, \]
independent of which leaves are corrupt. To bound a missed tamper fraction
$\rho$ at confidence $1-\delta$ it is enough to take
$k\ge \lceil \ln(1/\delta)/\rho\rceil$ approximately.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The probability that a without-replacement sample of size $k$ avoids all $m$
inconsistent leaves is $\binom{n-m}{k}/\binom{n}{k}$. Sampling without
replacement only increases detection relative to the independent bound
$(1-\rho)^k$. \end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Audit cost is $O(k)$ embeddings with $k\ll n$, giving trust in arbitrarily large
banked compute at sublinear marginal cost. The bound weakens gracefully as
$\rho$ shrinks and is vacuous for a single-leaf forgery, which is the price of
sampling.
\end{remark}

\section{What the artifact does and does not bind}
\label{sec:adv}
\begin{proposition}[Scope; the ``artifact is the answer'' is void]
\label{prop:void}
The commitment binds (a) corpus identity via $r_{\mathcal D}$ and (b) embedding
fidelity under $E$ via $r$ and the audit. It binds no property of any downstream
generator. For any two generators $G\neq G'$ consuming identical retrieved
context, $G$ and $G'$ may emit different outputs, so no function of $r$ fixes a
task output. Banked \emph{retrieval} is therefore not banked \emph{reasoning}.
\end{proposition}

We state the guarantee against an explicit hierarchy.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
\item[$\mathcal A_1$ transport tamperer] flips bytes of a stored artifact.
Defeated by Proposition~\ref{prop:integrity}.
\item[$\mathcal A_2$ lazy forger] commits self-consistent vectors without
running $E$. Defeated by Proposition~\ref{prop:audit}, not by integrity.
\item[$\mathcal A_3$ corpus substituter] swaps source text but re-embeds
honestly. Detected by comparing $r_{\mathcal D}$ to an independent anchor.
\item[Out of scope] an adversary who honestly runs $E$ on a corpus of their
choosing. \textsc{amber} authenticates the map, not the truthfulness of the
inputs. This is the exact boundary at which an external $r_{\mathcal D}$ anchor,
such as a signed release or a transparency log, becomes necessary.
\end{description}

\section{Related work}
Retrieval-augmented generation with offline vector stores is well established and
is not claimed here as novel. \textsc{amber} adds a verifiable, reproducible,
portable commitment layer over it. Merkle trees give the integrity primitive;
vector commitments and authenticated data structures give the position binding.
The audit is a sampling argument in the spirit of proofs of retrievability and
provable data possession, specialized to the predicate that the stored vector is
the pinned map's image of the stored chunk. Content-addressed datasets and
data-version tools hash artifacts but do not audit the computation relating two
committed layers, which is the gap Proposition~\ref{prop:audit} fills.

\section{Limitations}
The default embedder is lexical, not semantic; retrieval quality needs a pinned
transformer, which trades $\tau=0$ for a small tolerance and a revision pin. The
audit assumes the auditor can run $E$. It certifies fidelity to a map, not the
quality or truthfulness of the corpus. Corpus authenticity needs an external
anchor. Quantization is lossy, and inputs near cell boundaries can inflate
$\tau$. None of these affect Propositions \ref{prop:integrity}, \ref{prop:audit},
and \ref{prop:void}, which concern binding rather than retrieval quality.
